## Key Gaps

- **"Founding" TPM = building from scratch.** This is the company's first TPM hire. Erick's experience is operating within established PMOs and delivery structures (Verisk, Sanofi). Building a program management function from zero — defining processes, tooling, templates, governance, and team rituals for a company that has never had a TPM — is a qualitatively different challenge than running programs within an existing framework. This is the highest-risk gap.
- **Client-facing program ownership.** The role requires owning delivery for Supabase's largest clients, translating their technical requirements into specs, and managing client-facing timelines. Erick's delivery experience is internal (Verisk programs served internal business units; Sanofi programs served internal R&D). Customer-facing delivery — managing external client expectations, contractual commitments, and client escalation paths — is a distinct skill set.
- **Technical spec translation.** "Translate client technical requirements into actionable specs" suggests engineering-adjacent depth. Erick's strength is delivery management, not requirements engineering or technical specification authorship. This task typically sits with product managers or solutions architects, not TPMs — the expectation here signals a smaller org where the TPM wears multiple hats.
- **Async-first culture.** Supabase is a globally distributed, async-first organization. Erick's experience is in synchronous enterprise environments (sprint ceremonies, PI Planning, CABs, daily standups). Adapting delivery governance to async workflows — written decision-making, async standups, timezone-distributed reviews — requires a cultural shift.
- **Unknown compensation.** Without disclosed comp, there is risk of the offer falling below the $100K FTE floor, especially if the "founding" role frame is used to weight equity over base.
- **No regulated-environment overlap.** CS-7 does not engage. Erick's pharma/compliance differentiator provides no competitive advantage here.
