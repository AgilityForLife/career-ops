## Gaps — where he loses

- **"Genuine passion for AI — follow the space closely, experiment with tools personally."** Erick has practical AI tool experience at BTII but is not an AI domain expert. He uses AI tools for business process automation; this role wants someone who lives and breathes AI as a domain. The gap is between "uses AI tools effectively" and "is an AI adoption thought leader."
- **AI use case identification and prioritization requires AI product understanding.** "Identify and prioritize AI use cases using structured framework" demands understanding what AI can and cannot do across business functions — sales, marketing, operations, finance, product. This goes beyond PM tooling and into AI strategy.
- **Change management is the core function, not technical delivery.** "Lead company-wide change management program to shift workflows toward AI-generated/AI-assisted work." This is fundamentally a change management/transformation role. Erick's strength is technical platform delivery, not organizational behavior change. The SPC6 transformation work helps, but that was agile transformation, not AI adoption transformation.
- **De-emphasis concern.** Per skills-model de-emphasis rules: role may lean toward Tier 4 territory (coaching, training, change management) disguised as Program Manager title. The core deliverable is changing how people work, not delivering a technical platform. Monitor — does not trigger the 3.0 cap because the program structure dimension (CS-1) is genuine, but it's a positioning risk.
- **No fintech/credit-score domain experience.** SavvyMoney is a credit monitoring and financial wellness platform. No direct financial services delivery on Erick's resume, though Verisk (insurance analytics) provides adjacent industry credibility.
- **CS-2 absent.** The primary technical differentiator (cloud/data platform delivery) does not surface. This role doesn't need infrastructure modernization — it needs someone who can convince a marketing team to use AI.
