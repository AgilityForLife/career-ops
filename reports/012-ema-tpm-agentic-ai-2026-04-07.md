## Fit Analysis

### Strengths

- **Enterprise delivery at scale directly transfers.** Erick's Verisk experience (5 teams, 50+ engineers, 4 releases/year) maps to orchestrating complex cross-functional AI platform delivery. The "enterprise customers" focus aligns with his background serving enterprise stakeholders.
- **Cross-functional orchestration is a core match.** The role requires partnering across Engineering, Product, and AI Application teams — directly parallel to Erick's coordination across Dev, QA, Platform, and Analytics teams at Verisk and data engineering, MDM, analytics at Sanofi.
- **Cloud platform delivery experience is relevant.** While the JD doesn't specify AWS/Snowflake, Erick's hands-on delivery of cloud infrastructure (golden AMIs, EKS/ECS Fargate, Terraform) provides credibility when managing AI platform engineering teams.
- **PMP + PMI-ACP + SPC6 certification stack signals maturity.** For a startup scaling to enterprise customers, having a TPM with enterprise delivery discipline is valuable — brings structure without bureaucracy.
- **AI-Adjacent PM framing applies.** Per interview prep rules: lead with CS-1 (enterprise delivery at scale) + CS-4 (data-driven reporting), bridge with "applying program delivery discipline to AI adoption and tooling."

### Gaps

- **No direct AI/ML delivery experience.** Erick has no track record delivering AI/ML models, LLM applications, or agentic AI systems. This is the most significant gap. Competitors for this role will likely have AI-specific delivery experience.
- **Startup culture fit uncertainty.** Erick's experience is entirely in large enterprises (Verisk, Sanofi, Comcast/DSG) and mid-size organizations. Startup pace, ambiguity tolerance, and flat hierarchy dynamics are untested.
- **No explicit cloud/data platform match in JD.** CS-2 is only partially engaged because the JD focuses on AI platform delivery, not cloud infrastructure modernization specifically. Score does not get the full CS-2 credit.
- **Power BI / executive reporting not leveraged.** CS-4 is not engaged — no mention of dashboards or reporting. A key differentiator goes unused.
- **SAFe is likely irrelevant.** Startups rarely adopt SAFe; CS-6 provides no competitive advantage here.
- **Regulated environment experience not valued.** CS-7 does not apply in a startup AI context — another differentiator that doesn't transfer.
